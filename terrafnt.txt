1. WHAT IS THIS GAME?

Terra Front is a shoot-'em-up for one player. At the bottom of the
screen stands the front: up to four fighter ships side by side, moving
left and right as one. Waves of enemies loop and swoop overhead; break
up their pairs, shoot them down, dodge their bullets, and call up
reserve ships to fill the gaps in the front.


2. WHAT YOU NEED

- Any MSX computer: MSX1, MSX2, MSX2+ or turbo R. The game was tested
  on a Gradiente Expert 1.1 (a Brazilian MSX1), a 60 Hz machine, and on
  a Philips NMS 8250 (a European MSX2), a 50 Hz machine. It plays at
  the same speed on both.
- The file TERRAFNT.ROM, a 32 KB cartridge image. Or, on an MSX with a
  disk drive, the disk image TERRAFNT.DSK or the file TERRAFNT.BIN (see
  below).
- The keyboard, or a joystick in either port.


3. HOW TO RUN IT

On a real MSX with a disk drive, from a floppy disk:
  TERRAFNT.DSK is the image of a 720K (double-sided) disk that starts
  the game by itself. Write it to a floppy with a disk image tool, put
  the disk in drive A and switch on.

On a real MSX with a disk drive, from Disk BASIC:
  TERRAFNT.BIN is the game as a BASIC binary file. Copy it to any disk
  (or SD card or hard disk, with Nextor) and type
        BLOAD"TERRAFNT.BIN",R
  The disk version needs 32 KB of RAM.

On a real MSX, with a ROM launcher:
  SofaRun, EXECROM, ODO, LOADROM or the ROM loaders of flash cartridges
  (Carnivore2, MegaFlashROM SCC+ and others) load TERRAFNT.ROM and start
  it as if it were a cartridge.

On a real MSX, as a real cartridge:
  The game is a plain 32 KB ROM at addresses 4000h-BFFFh, with no
  mapper: burn it into a 27C256 EPROM on a blank MSX cartridge board
  that maps it there.

On a PC, Mac or phone, with an emulator:
  Any MSX emulator can run it: openMSX, blueMSX, fMSX, WebMSX and
  others. With openMSX, for example:
        openmsx -carta terrafnt.rom
  or, on an emulated MSX with a disk drive:
        openmsx -machine Philips_NMS_8250 -diska terrafnt.dsk


4. CONTROLS

                    KEYBOARD     JOYSTICK (either port)
  Move ............ Left/Right   Stick left/right
  Fire ............ Space        Button 1 (A)
  Call up a ship .. C            Button 2 (B)
  Pause ........... F1 or STOP   -

Each press of fire shoots one volley; holding it down doesn't keep
firing. At most two volleys fly at a time.

F1 or STOP pauses the game ("PAUSED" shows at the top of the screen);
press either again to go on.

On the title screen, press the space bar or a joystick's button 1 to
start a game. Left alone for 20 seconds, the title gives way to the
high score list for 7 seconds, then shows the game playing itself for a
while; press fire to come back to the title.


5. HOW TO PLAY

The front: every ship in it fires at once, one bullet from each nose.
When a ship is hit by an enemy bullet, or an enemy crashes into it, it
blows up and leaves a hole in the front.

Reserve ships: they wait in the bottom left corner (four at the start).
Press C to call one up: it flies in from the side of the screen away
from the front and joins it on that side. Meanwhile the front can shoot
but can't move. A front can hold four ships, holes included. When the
front is wiped out, the next reserve ship comes in by itself. With no
reserve left, the game is over.

Enemies: they come in waves, flying a loop in pairs. Hit a pair and it
breaks up: its two enemies fly off alone, bouncing off the top and the
bottom of the playfield. Shoot them down, or the front will have to
dodge them. They shoot back, more and faster as the waves go by.

Levels: a level is 10 waves. After wave 5 of each level comes a boss
that cruises along the top of the screen, takes many hits and rains
bullets on the front. After wave 10 comes another: it stays put at the
top, behind a line of enemy pairs that swerves left and right, and
sends down new pairs as the old ones fall. Its pairs break up and score
as in a wave, and it shoots too. When it is destroyed, every enemy and
bullet left vanishes.

Bonus ship: finish two waves in a row without losing a ship, and a
reserve ship is added (five at most).


6. SCORING

  Breaking up a pair ............ 50
  Shooting down a lone enemy .... 100
  An enemy crashing into a ship . 100
  Destroying a boss ............. 500 bonus

These are for a front of one ship. A bigger front scores less: 80% with
two ships, 60% with three, 50% with four. The score is at the top right;
the level and wave at the top left.

Key 1, during a game, skips to the level's next boss. It is there for
playtesting.


7. HIGH SCORES

The high score list holds the ten best scores, each with three
initials. At power-on it reads LTR 5000, EHE 4800, TEN 4500, SAA 4000,
ALZ 3500, SII 3000, SES 2500, UNT 2000, MSO 1500, EAO 1000. The title
shows the best score at the right, under "HI SCORE".

A score makes the list if it beats one of them (on a tie, the older
score stays above). At the end of that game the list comes up under
"ENTER YOUR INITIALS", with a blinking cursor where yours go:

- On the keyboard, type the letters A to Z. Backspace takes the last
  one back. RETURN (ENTER) ends the entry.
- With a joystick or the arrow keys, left and right pick a letter (held,
  they keep going; right starts at A, left at Z), and either button or
  the space bar takes it. Taking the third letter ends the entry. Up
  ends it too, and down takes the last letter back (straight up and
  down only, not diagonally).

One or two letters are fine: RETURN or up ends the entry once there is
at least one. The disk version keeps the list in the file TERRAFNT.HSC
on the default drive (normally the one it was started from), saving it
after each new entry; with no such file, the list starts as above.
